# Severity Ranking of Pharmaceutical Batch Scenarios

Thank you for taking part. This exercise takes about 20 minutes. Please
complete it on your own, without discussing it with anyone, and return the
response sheet (response_sheet.csv, or the table at the end of this document)
by email.

## What we are asking

Below are three products. For each product you will see eight scenarios
describing the state of one batch as it arrives at the pharmacy after passing
through five custody stages: manufacturer, importer, central warehouse,
distributor, pharmacy. Each scenario describes what the records show. Please
rank the eight scenarios for each product from

- 1 = least concern for drug integrity and patient safety at the point of
  dispensing, to
- 8 = greatest concern.

Use your professional judgment. There are no right answers we are checking
against. Ties are allowed if you consider two scenarios equally concerning,
but please avoid them where you can. Do not look up the scenarios; rank them
as described.

Please also tell us your professional role (for example community
pharmacist, hospital pharmacist, quality assurance specialist, regulatory
affairs) and your years of experience. We will report rankers by role only.

## Records available for every batch

- Serialization: every package carries a unique serial number that is
  scanned and verified at each custody transfer. The records show how many
  packages were scanned and how many failed verification.
- Temperature: a data logger records the storage temperature at regular
  intervals during each custody period. The records show how many readings
  fell outside the product's required range and by how much.
- Shelf life: the remaining shelf life on arrival, compared with the minimum
  remaining shelf life the regulator requires for pharmacy stock.
- Documentation: whether the required batch documents are present. The
  Certificate of Analysis (CoA), the GMP certificate and the batch release
  certificate are the critical ones; the bioequivalence study and the
  import licence are routine.
- Recall: whether the batch is under an active recall. No scenario below
  involves a recall.

Unless a scenario says otherwise, everything else about the batch is normal:
all packages scanned and verified, all temperature readings within range,
remaining shelf life well above the regulatory minimum, all documents
present, no recall.

## Product 1: Insulin (refrigerated biologic)

Required storage 2 to 8 C. Manufactured shelf life 24 months. Regulatory
minimum remaining shelf life for pharmacy stock: 180 days.

- **A.** At one custody transfer only 20 of the 100 packages in the batch
  were scanned; all 20 verified correctly. The other 80 were not verified at
  that stage.
- **B.** During one custody period, half of the temperature readings were at
  about 25 C (17 C above the upper limit). The other half were within range.
- **C.** All records normal.
- **D.** The Certificate of Analysis for the batch is missing from the
  importer's documentation. All other documents are present.
- **E.** During one custody period, 5 percent of the temperature readings
  were at about 10 C (2 C above the upper limit). The rest were within
  range.
- **F.** Two things happened in the same batch: at the warehouse, 5 percent
  of the temperature readings were at about 10 C (2 C above the upper
  limit); and at the distributor only 20 of the 100 packages were scanned
  (all 20 verified correctly).
- **G.** At the importer, 5 of the 100 packages failed serial-number
  verification (their serial numbers were not recognised as genuine). The
  other 95 verified correctly.
- **H.** The batch arrived at the pharmacy with 90 days of shelf life
  remaining (the regulatory minimum for pharmacy stock is 180 days).
